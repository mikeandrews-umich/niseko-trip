Backcountry gates

Niseko's gates open terrain beyond the boundary. Only go through with avalanche gear, a partner, and the know-how (or book a guided tour below). Gates close when posted, so respect the rules.

Spots to hit

Strawberry Fields (Hanazono) for trees, the Pizza Box lift, and Boyoso. Night skiing at Hirafu is included with your pass.

FAQs

Don't see your question? Ask in the group chat.

Which airport do I fly into?

New Chitose Airport (CTS) near Sapporo. Land by 3:00 PM on Sun Jan 3 to catch the 4:00 PM chartered bus to Niseko.

What if I'm arriving late or on a different day?

You'll need to get to Niseko on your own. Airport buses and private transfers run from CTS. Tell the logistics team your plans so we know when to expect you.

Do I need a visa?

U.S. passport holders don't need a visa for short tourist stays in Japan. Other passports vary, so check the Japan MOFA visa-exemption list early. International students: make sure your U.S. visa/I-20 paperwork is in order for re-entry.

Do I need an Ikon Pass?

Niseko United is on the Ikon Pass. Pass and lift-ticket details: TBA.

Ski or snowboard? I'm a beginner.

Either works. Optional morning and afternoon lessons will be offered. Sign-up details coming soon.

Money: cash or card?

Cards work in most of Hirafu, but bring some yen for small restaurants, buses and onsen. Konbini ATMs (Lawson, 7-Eleven) take foreign cards.

Will my phone charger work?

Yes. Japan uses the same Type A plugs as the U.S. (100V). Bring a portable battery; phones die fast in the cold.

What if I don't want to ski every day?

Plenty to do: onsen, tubing, the Rusutsu day trip, snowmobiles, or a slow morning in Hirafu. See Add-ons.
